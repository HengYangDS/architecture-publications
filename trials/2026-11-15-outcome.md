# Outcome of the roadmap's first step

Written 2026-11-15, after the last run. The first step of Architecture Publisher's roadmap wrote no product code:
it tested the works on paper and by experiment, with lines set beforehand, so that later steps build only what a
test supports. This page says what each trial decided and what follows; the records hold the numbers.

The stop line of the step was this: had neither arm with a work beaten the repository alone, investment in
presentation would have stopped and the product would have shrunk to checking, drift and a text projection. One arm
passed, so the stop line is not reached.

## What follows

- **A reader file in every delivery, in the form that passed.** What helped a reader was the work's own model and
  guide text beside the repository. The roadmap's reader file and data take that form.
- **Constraints, flows and decisions are not yet shown to be worth more than structure.** The one task tested asks
  where a change lands, which structure answers. Before the model gains written statements of other kinds, a task
  that such statements would answer has to be tested: whether a change is allowed, or what must stay true.
- **Plates serve people, and no person has been tested.** As images they did not help an agent reader. The plates'
  worth rests on the owner's looks at the sample book and at the six works.
- **Review of what a work says may be delegated; review of form may not be, by eye.** Each seeded defect of form
  has a number or a fact behind it that a program can read from the build. The form half of the reviewer's
  procedure will start from what the build measures, and the seeded check is run again on plates no holder has
  seen.
- **The line table admits** emphasis as two widths, a dashed boundary, and cited files as a number; it withdraws
  ticks and a hub told by colour alone; and it adds a test that every piece of one kind of line has one weight,
  after two pieces that fall on a pixel boundary were found drawn at a third of their weight.
- **An author's time goes to making a view fit a plate.** About fifty-five of ninety minutes went there, and a
  check gave no answer for ten minutes twice. The check needs a bound on its own time, and the roadmap's single
  anatomy and blocks by question are expected to remove the choices that cost the time; a second timing after them
  is what will tell.
- **Moving a work costs as much as writing it, when much has changed.** Thirty commits on, half the guide's
  citations had to be reread and the move did not finish in ninety minutes. The publisher names a shifted citation's
  new range and has no command that applies it. The works here are moved one release at a time, where few citations
  are touched.

## Found on the way

- The works answer about four fifths of [the question table](../questions/README.md)'s entries, and the guide alone
  about half: content is split between a work's model and its guide.
- [What the plates' marks mean today](screen/binding-table.md): of 81 visible differences 38 are bound to a meaning,
  20 are not, and 23 are unclear; thirteen type sizes are in use where four are declared.
- Five candidate defects of the published work on Film Kit, listed in the record of the false-statements check.
- Nearly half the words of a plate are set at 14 px in its drawing space, which is 11.2 px on a 1920 px page.

## Limits that apply to all of it

Every reader, reviewer and author here was an agent, of two origins at most. Works and repositories are one
owner's. Each trial ran once. Lines without precedent were set by committee seats. A holder from outside the
committee read the committee's design with the reader's and the author's records and wrote a dissent, which is kept
with Architecture Publisher's working records; its first point, that the publisher's own plate engine should be
compared with an established one before more is built on it, is taken up by a timing of the same author's task
without plates, to be recorded here when it has run.
