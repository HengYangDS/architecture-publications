## What it says, and what it does not

- An agent that had never seen the repository delivered a checked page from the Skill alone in under an hour. That
  is the first delivery of the three parts of this trial.
- It does not show that the plates are what cost the first author its ninety minutes. This holder ran one check
  with the plates in it, against its instructions, and that check passed at the first attempt: its first model fit
  where the other holder's did not. The two runs differ in the model each holder happened to write as much as in
  what was asked of them. What the pair does show is that fitting a view on a plate is where an author can lose most
  of its time, and that this depends on the first model's shape, which the author cannot see beforehand.
- The host was loaded through both runs: single checks took from 40 seconds to seven minutes. Minutes here are
  minutes on this host on this night.
- One repository, one holder, one run on each side.

## Follow-ups this record raises

- Before a first check, an author has no way to know whether a view will fit a plate. The refusal comes after the
  layout search; a measure of a view's size against a plate's room, reported by `check` before the search, would
  tell the author at once. For the queue's `one-anatomy` and `blocks-by-question` items.
- The dissent's request stands: a comparison of the publisher's plate engine with an established one on the same
  task is not answered by this run. It is part of the queue's `sample-book` item, which sets one page of the
  publisher's plates beside Archify's.
