## Fit of the table

Is more than a third of the entries not applicable? No. One of 13 is not applicable as worded
(data-and-interfaces). One more has a clause with no object (stack-and-deployment, "how is it kept running").
The table fits better than its software wording suggests, because this repository also has a change lifecycle,
checks, CI and releases. The misfit lies in the wording of single questions, not in the list as a whole.

Three states do not hold a compound question well. Ten of the 13 questions join two or three clauses with
"and". Four of the five partial entries are compound questions whose answer covers one clause and leaves
another thin or empty. A matrix that should show gaps needs either one clause per entry or a state per clause.

Questions to reword for a repository of guidance:

- data-and-interfaces: ask "What records does it keep, and through which entries do readers and maintainers
  reach it?" The work would then be cited through `decision-records`, `edition`, `openspec-workspace`,
  `repository-entry`, `agent-entry` and `task-map`.
- stack-and-deployment: ask "What is it written and checked with, how does it reach its readers, and what keeps
  it current?" The last clause would point at ownership and review cadence instead of operations. The work does
  not state the owner; the repository does at `docs/charter.md` 157:160.
- top-level-design: "depend on each other" should read "relate to each other". Topics route, hand over and
  constrain; they do not depend in the software sense.
- mechanisms: "its most important work" is unclear for guidance. Ask how a reader gets from a work question to
  the rule that governs it, and how a rule is changed.
- start-and-change: separate "where does a reader start" from "how does a maintainer make a change". They have
  different readers and different sources.
- context-and-goals: say whether "what must it achieve" means the stated purpose or the accepted requirements.
  This work answers the second and omits the first.
- decisions-and-risks: "risk" collides with the repository's own L0 to L2 risk levels. Ask "what is still open
  or unproved" if that is what is meant.

Questions to merge:

- start-and-change (its change half) and verification-and-release. In this work the same three guide sections
  (`#p-change`, `#p-checks`, `#p-release`) answer both, because one tool owns admission, proof and release.

Questions to add:

- Whom does the guidance bind, and with what force? The work has cited statements for this (`docs/charter.md`
  96:108, on rule words binding members and their Agents), but no entry asks for it.
- Who owns the guidance and who may change it? The work names owners of documents and tools. It does not name
  the accountable person, which the repository gives at `docs/charter.md` 157:160.
- Is the guidance in use, and what would show it? The work says in four cited statements that a readable
  route, checks, review or release do not show adoption (`docs/charter.md` 151:153; `docs/README.md` 78:88;
  `openspec/specs/work-practice/spec.md` 3:18; `openspec/specs/verification/spec.md` 10:22). For guidance this
  is the evaluator's main question, and the table has no entry for it.

Does "code size" mean anything here? Only as reading length, and only for prose. Measured at the pin, the 48
files hold 6,005 lines. The longest files are `CHANGELOG.md` (885), the work-practice spec (620), the
guidance-discovery spec (483) and `CONTRIBUTING.md` (382). The seven topics run from 131 lines (`docs/deliver.md`)
to 303 (`docs/data.md`). The parts a reader meets first are the shortest: `AGENTS.md` 16 lines, `README.md` 24,
`docs/README.md` 88, and the charter, which every topic applies, 164. Size therefore does not track weight or
complexity, and a short entry is a stated requirement (`openspec/specs/guidance-discovery/spec.md` 11:19).
For the configuration parts (several files of 1 to 16 lines) the figure carries no meaning. A count of words to
read per topic would mean something to a newcomer; a count of lines of "code" does not.

Does "file age" mean anything here? Little at this pin. By `git log -1 --format=%cs <pin> -- <path>`, 47 of
the 48 files were last changed between 2026-10-09 and 2026-11-01, and `LICENSE` on 2026-09-26. The history has
794 commits since 2026-07-10. Age by last commit barely separates the parts, and it counts a formatting change
the same as a change of rule. What would mean something for guidance is the edition in which a rule last
changed, read against the review cadence the guidance sets for itself. The changelog holds that; file age does
not.
