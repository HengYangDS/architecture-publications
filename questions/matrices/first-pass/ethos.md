## Observations

- The three-state rule saturates on this work. Every question has two or three parts, and "at least one
  statement with evidence" is met by answering one part. All 13 entries are cited, yet 3 leave a part
  with no cited statement and 3 more answer a part thinly. The state cannot tell a work that answers
  "how is a change verified" from one that also answers "how is a release made".
- `stack-and-deployment` was the hardest entry. One fact row citing `pyproject.toml` 1:6 carries "built
  with"; installation, distribution and dependencies are absent; "kept running" has no object for a
  command-line product. I recorded it as cited because the Python and platform facts do answer part of
  the question, not because the entry is covered.
- `start-and-change` and `terms` were hard for the same reason in a different form. The guide tells an
  adopter how to start in their own repository, not a contributor how to start in this one. Terms are
  defined in passing inside step tables; `concepts.md` is a rendering table, not a glossary.
- The overview page carries no citations. Under the evidence rule none of its sentences count, although it
  is the only place that states the release step (signed tag on `main`, hosted job), the write order and
  the lease verbs. Its masthead refers to the 515 hashed files, which is evidence for the model, not for
  the page's own sentences.
- A view cites nothing itself; its evidence is the files of its members. View `overview` can be read as an
  answer to `top-level-design`, `mechanisms` and `verification-and-release`; view `runtime` to
  `stack-and-deployment` and `data-and-interfaces`; view `structure` to `constraints-and-invariants` and,
  wrongly, to `repository-structure` (it describes what ETHOS demands of any repository, not where
  ETHOS's parts live). I did not count a view alone as the answer to `mechanisms`, because `limits` says
  relations are not runtime order.
- The guide's citations go mostly outside the model's coverage scope: 63 of 84 `@source` entries cite
  `docs/` (54), `README.md` (6), `CHANGELOG.md` (2) or `pyproject.toml` (1); 21 cite files under
  `src/ethos/`, `system/` or `.ethos/`. The work's README says the model leaves out documentation. The two halves
  of the work answer different questions from different evidence: the model answers `top-level-design` and
  `repository-structure`; the guide answers almost everything else.
- Non-structural content the repository holds and the work does not state: 14 of 16 current decision
  records (DR-0010, DR-0011, DR-0013 to DR-0024); the 15 numbered invariants as a list (PDC 588:602);
  the rule files under `rules/` (8 plus a README); 12 specifications under `openspec/specs/` with 603
  `### Requirement:` headings; the release procedure (`docs/guides/release-and-publication.md`); the
  contributor process (`CONTRIBUTING.md`); the distribution design (`docs/architecture/distribution.md`);
  the glossary (`docs/reference/glossary.md`).
- `constraints-and-invariants` has two subjects in a governance product: what must hold inside ETHOS, and
  what ETHOS makes every governed repository hold. The work answers both (principles and plan contract for
  the first, the paradigm section for the second). The question table does not separate them, so one
  state covers both.
- Question-table fit for this kind of repository: "how is it kept running" assumes a service;
  "start working on it" does not say whether "it" is the product in use or the repository under change;
  "risks" assumes the repository records risks as such, and this one records boundaries, open items and
  revisit conditions instead.
- `decisions-and-risks` and `changes-and-direction` each pair a well-evidenced half (one decision with its
  alternatives; fifteen dated changes) with a thin half (remaining risks; direction in two table rows).
  Splitting each into two entries would let the matrix show this.
