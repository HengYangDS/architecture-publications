## Fit of the table

Share of NOT APPLICABLE: 1 of 27, about 4 percent (`data`). In the first pass it was 1 of 13. Splitting
`data-and-interfaces` let `interfaces` be answered on its own.

What the split changed for this work: two gaps that the first table hid are now their own SILENT entries
(`direction`, `owned-by`). The first pass reported both only as notes on cited entries.

Entries that still join two clauses, where the clauses got different answers here:

- `goals`: "what must it achieve" is cited; "who answers for it" is not, and repeats `owned-by`. Drop the
  second clause.
- `owned-by`: "who owns" is silent; "may change it" is cited and repeats `changing-it`. Ask only who is
  accountable for the guidance.
- `in-use`: "is it in use" has a cited answer of "not shown"; "how is that known" has none. Consider two
  entries, or ask "what would show that it is in use".

Wording still wrong for a repository of guidance:

- `data`: "What data does it keep, and where?" does not apply. "What records does it keep, and where?" would be
  cited through `decision-records`, `edition` and `openspec-workspace`.
- `interfaces`: the work's word is "entry". "Through which entries is it read and maintained, and how does it
  reach others?" matches what the model draws.
- `dependencies`: topics route, hand over and constrain. "How do the parts relate to each other?" fits.
- `installed-and-run`: "where does it run" has no object. "How does it reach its readers?" is the live half.
- `mechanisms`: "its most important work" had to be read as two things, a reader finding a rule and a rule
  changing. For guidance, name the first one.
- `invariants` and `never-break`: answered by the same requirements here. For guidance the useful split is
  between what the guidance demands of the department's work and what its maintenance must keep. The table
  does not ask the first; `binds-whom` covers its scope and force but not its content.
- `open-risks`: the repository uses "risk" for its own L0 to L2 levels of department work. The entry's
  "limits or open items" is the part that fits.
- `terms`: fits as worded, and matters more here than for software. One cited definition is enough to make the
  entry CITED, so the state does not show that the work has no glossary.

The three guidance entries all apply. `binds-whom` and `in-use` found cited statements that no entry of the
first table asked for. `owned-by` found a real gap: the repository names an owner and the work does not.
